Priya,

Thanks for sending the Northgate letter. I've reviewed it with our data governance team.
Here is where we come out:

1. **Standard access.** Everything you are entitled to under Section 9.3 is available today
   in the Meridian Insight portal. You can pull the standard de-identified extract at any time;
   it includes service category, provider type, geography, paid amounts and member cost share.
   That is electronic access to de-identified claims data, which is what the CAA requires.

2. **Custom extract.** The Northgate field list asks for claim-level allowed amounts and
   provider names/NPIs. Those fields are not in the standard extract because allowed amounts
   and provider-level rates are Meridian Proprietary Information under the ASA. We can
   produce a custom extract under Section 9.3 - the fee would be $18,500 plus $0.11 per claim
   line (for 36 months I'd estimate 1.1-1.3 million lines, so roughly $140-160k all-in), and
   our standard delivery window is 180 days from acceptance. I'll be candid that our data
   governance team is also reviewing whether a 36-month, all-claims extract with provider
   identifiers would reveal our network pricing in a way that we can decline under 9.3.

3. **Northgate.** Northgate offers benefits consulting, which makes it a "Competitor" under
   the ASA, and Meridian's consulting affiliate competes with them for the same work. We will
   not consent to Northgate receiving Claims Data under Section 9.4. That is not a close call
   for us. If Halvorsen wants benchmarking, Meridian Advisory Services can provide a network
   performance report at no charge.

4. **Claimsight.** We have no objection in principle to Claimsight performing the Section 9.6
   audit once it signs our Third-Party Data Use Agreement. Per 9.6 the audit sample is 250
   claims, drawn using our methodology, reviewed in our secure audit portal. If Claimsight
   wants a larger sample or claim-level pricing fields, that would need to be a separately
   scoped (and priced) engagement.

5. **The gag clause question.** I know your consultant is going to raise the CAA. We've
   looked at this closely with counsel across our book of business. The statute says plans
   can't agree to restrictions on electronically accessing de-identified data; it does not say
   anything about format, frequency, turnaround time, or price, and it does not give employers
   the right to hand our proprietary rates to our competitors. Charging for custom work and
   protecting proprietary information are commercial terms, not gag clauses. And in any event
   Section 9.7 says nothing in Article 9 can be read to restrict your access in a way the law
   prohibits - so by definition there is no gag clause in this agreement. Every one of our
   self-funded clients attests on that basis.

Happy to get on a call with your Committee if useful.

Tom

Tom Delacroix
Senior Account Executive, Meridian Health Administrators, LLC
